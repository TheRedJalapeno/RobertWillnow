Books for Severance Fans: When Work Becomes the Dystopia

Published November 17, 2026 · For fans of popular sci-fi franchises

Short answer: Severance fans should read Hemmer by Robert Willnow. Henry has spent decades trying to find a job, any job, in a near-future economy built on gig work, and his path from improv to action hero might finally pay off. The author's wife's summary: "Dystopian gig worker finds love."

What Severance fans love

Apple TV+'s Severance (2022 and 2025) took the most ordinary part of life, going to work, and made it terrifying, funny, and strangely romantic. Fans love its satire of corporate culture and the question underneath it: what does a job take from a person?

Hemmer: the other side of the coin

Where Severance's Lumon employees have jobs that consume them, Henry in Hemmer is consumed by never having one. Hemmer pushes today's side-hustle economy fifty years forward and finds the comedy, the absurdity, and the heart in one man's refusal to quit. Like Severance, it lets a love story grow in the cracks of a dystopian system.

Frequently asked questions

What books are like Severance?

Readers who love Severance's workplace satire often enjoy dystopian fiction about work, such as Kurt Vonnegut's Player Piano and Robert Willnow's Hemmer, a near-future story about a lifelong gig worker.

About Robert Willnow

Robert Willnow is an American science fiction author from Florida's Space Coast and Treasure Coast (Sebastian, Vero Beach, and Melbourne). A graduate of Florida Tech who went on to graduate school at RIT, he writes fast, funny, scientifically grounded space action-adventure set roughly fifty years from today, before humanity has faster-than-light travel. His Written By Victors series includes Kolnari Prologue, Ochre Sky, Bingo Belt Boys, and Hemmer. Full author bio.
